Abstract

Citizen service delivery in the State of Maharashtra under the legacy Aaple Sarkar portal suffers from chronic administrative bottlenecks, discretionary clerk delays, repetitive document uploads (up to 14 times per financial year), and rampant physical photocopy forgery. Although the Maharashtra Right to Public Services (RTS) Act, 2015 establishes statutory delivery timelines, existing digital portals lack automated enforcement, resulting in average turnaround times of 15 to 45 days and pervasive rent-seeking.

This project introduces MahaGov-Pulse, an enterprise-grade Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) operating under a Decoupled Trust Model. The system integrates: (1) A sovereign "Once-Only" MahaVault using AES-256-GCM envelope encryption and salted SHA-256 identity hashing; (2) A Deterministic Statutory Policy Engine (Open Policy Agent - OPA) codifying the Maharashtra Land Revenue Code (MLRC) 1966, BARTI/TRTI deemed date regulations, and EWS asset ceilings; (3) An Anti-Corruption Blind Algorithmic Dispatcher (HMAC-SHA256) that breaks middleman collusion; (4) A 48-Hour Hard SLA Escrow Engine with automated SDO escalation; (5) W3C Verifiable Credentials signed via ECDSA over the SECP256R1 (P-256) curve, embedded in offline-scannable QR codes; and (6) An Autonomous MahaDBT Welfare Scheme Matcher connecting issued certificates directly to welfare benefits.

Chapter 1: Introduction

1.1 Background & Problem Context

In Maharashtra, access to higher education (MHT-CET / NEET CAP admission rounds), public employment via the Maharashtra Public Service Commission (MPSC), and welfare subsidies depends entirely upon statutory certificates issued by the Revenue, Social Justice, and Home departments.

Under the existing portal (Aaple Sarkar), citizens face severe obstacles:

  • Repetitive Document Uploads: Citizens are forced to upload the exact same 7/12 extract and income proof up to 14 separate times every year.
  • Arbitrary Clerk Delays: Clerks hold files for 21 to 45 days until citizens visit local Setu offices or pay informal facilitation fees.
  • Photocopy Forgery: Because paper certificates rely on physical ink stamps, forged certificates circulate widely in educational admissions.

Chapter 5: Mathematical Modeling & Cryptography

5.1 Blind Algorithmic Dispatch

To prevent applicants from colluding with specific local clerks, application routing is modeled as a cryptographically seeded pseudo-random permutation over the active officer set:

$$\text{Seed} = \text{HMAC-SHA256}(K_{\text{state}}, \text{ApplicantID} \parallel \text{Timestamp}_{\text{hour}})$$
$$\text{AssignedOfficerIndex} = \text{Seed} \pmod N$$

Where $K_{\text{state}}$ is the secret key held by the state KMS, and $N$ is the number of active Nayab Tahsildars in the district. Neither the citizen nor the officer can predict or manipulate assignment.

5.2 ECDSA P-256 Verifiable Credentials

Digital certificates are minted as canonical W3C JSON-LD credentials signed using ECDSA over the SECP256R1 curve:

$$\text{Signature} = \text{ECDSA-Sign}(d_{\text{officer}}, \text{SHA256}(\text{CanonicalCredential}))$$

This produces a 64-byte compact signature easily embedded within a Level-M 2D QR code printed on physical certificates. Universities and employers verify validity completely offline:

$$\text{Verify}(K_{\text{public}}, \text{SHA256}(\text{CanonicalCredential}), \text{Signature}) \in \{\text{True}, \text{False}\}$$
